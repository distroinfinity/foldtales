# AI Storyteller: what the AI side becomes (concept, Nov 14)

**The product is two ways** (owner, Nov 14): **Tales from the Table's Edge**, the authored book of chapters, and the **AI Storyteller** (names from the product workspace). This is the AI Storyteller. The owner asked for something bolder than "free play plus a show": more immersion, more playground, built on what AI unlocks right now, not limited by the PRD. Research behind it: `docs/plans/2026-11-14-ai-mode-research.md`.

## The idea in one line

**The cast steps off the page and into your evening.** They hear you and answer in their own voices, they notice your real room and the real things you show them, anything you imagine can appear on the table, and the story is made with you, out loud, as it happens.

## What AI unlocks that nothing else on the Quest does

1. **They hear you, and answer like actors.** Talk naturally, interrupt, laugh. Pip, Wick and Moth answer at once, each in their own voice (ElevenLabs, streaming in about a quarter of a second), mouths moving, acting as they speak. No text, no menus. This is the floor everything else stands on.
2. **The AI stages, not just speaks.** Every reply can move the world: walk Pip to the edge, turn the page to the shore, dim the lights to night, light the lamp, sweep the beam over your room, open the drawer. The director's stage actions already exist; here they're driven by you, not a script.
3. **Your room walks into the story.** The Quest knows your table, couch, walls and windows (Space Setup's labelled planes, available in the browser). The cast can use them: "Look, a great soft mountain!" (your couch), Moth flies to your real window, Pip hops off the book onto your real table and walks to its edge to look down.
4. **Show them something real.** Hold up your mug. Pip gasps: "The Giant's cup!" A painted storybook version of it appears (camera frame, then vision, then a painting in the book's style, then a cut-out: about 30 s, covered by Moth flying off to fetch it), and tonight's story is about *your* lost thing. *The Quest's browser reports a camera device to our page; whether it hands us the passthrough image needs one quick test in the headset.*
5. **Anything you imagine can appear.** Say "a teapot that lost its whistle": Moth fetches a painted teapot, and you hear its little broken whistle (ElevenLabs sound effects, made on the spot). The music shifts with the scene.
6. **You're in the cast.** With your permission, a 30-second sample of your voice makes a new character, "the Giant", who speaks your lines in the story, in your voice. (Or someone you love reads as the narrator.) Consent first, deletable, never shared.
7. **They remember you.** Next evening: "You're back! Did you ever find your cup?" Who you are, what you made together, and what's in your satchel carry over.

## How an evening goes

1. You open the AI Storyteller. The book opens on a blank spread with the three of them waiting, and the lamp low.
2. Pip looks up: "Oh! It's you. What shall we make tonight?" Wick grumbles; Moth flutters at your hand.
3. You say anything, show anything, or hand them something. They take it and run: the page turns to a set, the light changes, someone walks, something appears.
4. Every so often the story turns to you ("Should we go down to the shore, or up to the lamp?"), and your answer decides the next scene. Or you just watch them play it out.
5. You can stop them any time ("Wait, what if…"), and the story bends then and there.
6. When it winds down, the narrator gathers it into a page of the book, with what you made, and the lamp's beam sweeps your room.

## The hero moment (for the demo video and judges)

You hold up your real coffee mug. Pip gasps, "The Giant's cup!" Moth zips off and comes back with a painted version of it. Wick squints: "Hmph. Fell off the edge of the table, I expect." The three of them build a story about the Giant's lost cup, in their own voices, and turn to ask you what to do.

## Honest limits

- **Time:** four days to Nov 18. Pillars 1 and 2 are the core (two days). 3 and 5 are about half a day each. 4 needs the camera test first. 6 is half a day plus consent design. 7 builds on the memory already written.
- **Look bar:** anything painted live must pass it. Bad paintings are rejected automatically, and Moth "couldn't find it" rather than showing a bad one.
- **Cost per evening:** voices about $0.30, speech about $0.03, Claude a few cents, each live painting 2 credits (capped per evening).
- **Safety:** what you say and what's painted are checked; off-tone requests get an in-story redirect. Your voice is only cloned with an explicit yes, and can be deleted.
