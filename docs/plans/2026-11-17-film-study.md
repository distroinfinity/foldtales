# What makes an XR film land: a study for the Foldtales film

Lane S, Nov 17, 2026. Feeds `docs/plans/2026-11-17-film-concept.md`.

**How the references were checked.** Each video was downloaded, sampled at one frame a second, cut-detected, and its YouTube captions and description read; speech-to-text found which ones have narration. Timings are ±1 s, and cut counts are a lower bound, because soft dissolves don't register. Music styles are named only where YouTube's own caption labels name them. Devpost's pages were read through a reader proxy, because they block direct loads. Nothing here comes from memory alone.

## The references

### 1. Moss (Polyarc): the E3 2017 reveal and Book II's Quest trailer

Reveal: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8FguiwVNCs0 (2:00). Book II: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jkXWbu2B-jY (1:25). UploadVR on the premise ("you're the reader of the story"): https://uploadvr.com/moss-vr-magical-preview/

- **First 5 s:** spent on a rating card and platform logos. The first real image, at 6 s, is a slow move through a dark library, with whispering voices. The hook comes at 13–17 s: a glowing orb (you) touches an open book, and its page becomes a forest.
- **Throughline:** wonder, then a bond with a small character, then courage. The key beat is at about 65–72 s: the player's masked spirit, the Reader, seen reflected in a pond beside Quill. Book II is built on the same idea: "the ones who stand behind them".
- **Hands:** never shown. The player is an orb and a mask.
- **Sound:** the reveal has no narration, only music and the forest. Book II has one storybook narrator throughout.
- **Pacing:** long opening takes (10–30 s), a burst of sub-second cuts at 74–94 s, then a slow end. The title only arrives at about 105 s.
- **Text:** a studio card and the logo; Book II adds "Captured on PC".
- **Ending:** the logo, then a platform slogan and an end card.
- **Forced:** the logo stings at both ends and "Greatness Awaits"; Book II's narration is a generic hero monologue.
- **For us:** the closest premise. The player is the Reader, and the character knows you're there. Moss shows it with a reflection; our cast say it out loud ("Someone very big").

### 2. First Hand (Meta's hand-tracking showcase)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E6IWiDQ7csw (Popup Asylum for Meta, 0:57)

- **First 5 s:** a fade up on a dim hangar, then a level-select panel. It opens on UI, a weak hook.
- **Throughline:** "look what your hands can do". A list, with no story.
- **Hands:** all POV. Mostly rendered gloves; the mixed-reality part (about 29–35 s) shows a real hand holding a virtual part against a real wall.
- **Sound:** music only.
- **Pacing:** about 15 cuts in 57 s, one every 3–6 s. The title only at 52–57 s.
- **Ending:** the logo and the Quest logo, no call to action.
- **Forced:** no interaction is allowed to finish before the cut.
- **For us:** the counter-example. A hand mechanic only lands if the shot lets it complete: reach, take, give, and the reaction.

### 3. LEGO Bricktales, the Quest announce teaser

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ATfA7UWvbto (1:02)

- **First 5 s:** 1 s inside the virtual jungle diorama, then at 2 s the same diorama standing on a real coffee table in a living room. The whole hook is "this world is on your table".
- **Throughline:** "you built this", told backwards with a VHS "REWIND <<" overlay, from finished worlds back to the player putting the headset on.
- **Hands:** POV with Touch controllers; one third-person shot of a man on a sofa in a Quest 3, with a "CLICK" sound word.
- **Sound:** music, no narration.
- **Pacing:** 22 cuts in 62 s, about one every 2.7 s.
- **Text:** "REWIND <<", "CLICK", and a card saying "START BUILDING" in heavy sans.
- **Ending:** the logo, a release date, a pre-order offer, publishers' logos.
- **Forced:** the imperative slogan card and the pre-order line.
- **For us:** the diorama-on-a-real-table image is the hook; we have a better version of it, because ours moves out into the room.

### 4. Cubism, the mixed-reality trailer

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VV28TQyno-c (0:42)

- **First 5 s:** a block against a sunset, a knitted sleeve and hand reaching for it, then by 3 s a woman on a sofa turning the puzzle with her bare hands.
- **Throughline:** calm and tactile; warm daylight, pastel.
- **Hands:** all third-person composites of the player, bare hands, no POV at all.
- **Pacing:** almost all soft dissolves; one hard cut.
- **Sound:** music only, then silence for the last 4 s.
- **Text:** none but the logo, assembled from blocks.
- **Ending:** a platform end card, "Available Now".
- **For us:** the closest tone, and proof that calm can carry a trailer. Its method (studio composites) is ruled out by our real-footage rule; its silence at the end is not.

### 5. "What If…? An Immersive Story" for Apple Vision Pro

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lLk-IkPktMo (Marvel / ILM Immersive, 1:42). An Apple-published trailer for Encounter Dinosaurs wasn't found; only third-party reviews.

- **First 5 s:** three studio logo cards, then the narrator: "Time… space… reality…"
- **Throughline:** "you have been chosen". The multiverse breaks and spills into your living room.
- **The room transformed:** a character steps out of a portal into a real-looking loft, held as one shot for about 36 s.
- **Hands:** POV hands casting spells; live-action close-ups of a smiling person in the headset. Whether the room shots are device captures isn't stated.
- **Sound:** narration and character dialogue throughout.
- **Pacing:** slow in the middle, then a montage of one-second cuts; the title at about 90 s.
- **Ending:** the title, a date, the platform.
- **Forced:** three logo cards before anything happens, and the smiling-face-in-a-headset reaction shot.
- **For us:** the long hold on one moment of the room changing (36 s) is what sticks, not the montage. The character speaking to you in your room is the same idea as our cast addressing the Reader.

### 6. Winners of the 2025 Meta Horizon Start competition

Winners: https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/meta-horizon-start-developer-competition-meet-the-winners/

**The Nanauts** (Best IWSDK experience). https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7r-29fp4Nbg, 1:18; https://devpost.com/software/nanauts
- **Format:** raw headset POV, square.
- **First 5 s:** a real hallway; an incoming call floats in it; a real hand taps to answer; a voice sets up the story in one sentence.
- **The strongest real-room moment** (11–14 s): the player pets their real dog while a little robot hops beside it.
- **Sound and captions:** the app's own voices and its own subtitles.
- **Pacing:** about five cuts: essentially one playthrough.
- **Ending:** no title or end card; it ends on the characters waving goodbye.
- **For us:** honest, story-led footage with no gloss won the IWSDK prize. The app's own voice and captions were enough.

**DreamSpace** (Best Entertainment). https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ogtDlO9M6jA, 3:50; https://devpost.com/software/dreamspace-6g8rzm
- **First 5 s:** a floating panel playing a clip from a Pixar film, with its audio.
- **The room:** a real bedroom whose ceiling becomes sky, walls with sunset windows, a storm at sea.
- **Structure:** a feature label on each section ("New Window Creation System"), long takes.
- **Ending:** a brush-script logo, "Available on Meta Quest Store".
- **Caveats:** it may not be the cut the judges saw; it's over three minutes; it shows a copyrighted film, which the 2026 rules forbid.
- **For us:** the Entertainment winner did room effects (sky on the ceiling, a storm) too. Our edge has to be that ours are part of a story, told in its own voice, not features with labels.

## What Meta and Devpost say

**Devpost, quoted exactly** (https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/, `/details/judging`, `/rules`):
- "A demonstration video—under three (3) minutes, showcasing footage of your Project as viewed on a Meta VR device, via XR Simulator, or another equivalent emulator."
- "Judges are not required to watch beyond three minutes, so lead with your best material."
- Polish & Presentation: "show real gameplay that honestly represents the experience", "Lead with the story and what makes your project distinct", "Don't lean on AI-generated video to carry the pitch."
- Experience Design: "passthrough should be purposeful… The player's real room should meaningfully change the experience… rather than a scene that plays identically with passthrough off."
- §4: "Projects must be entirely in the English language or include English subtitles."; "must not contain any commercial or corporate advertising… recognizable branded products, or commercial artwork."; "may not mention or depict any identifiable person other than Entrants."
- There's no separate captions rule and no aspect-ratio rule. Our own docs' "English captions when anyone speaks" is stricter than the rules; we keep it anyway, because it's right for judges watching muted.

**Meta's store asset guidelines** (https://developers.meta.com/horizon/resources/asset-guidelines/; VRC.Quest.Asset.7 and .10):
- 30 s to 2 min for a store trailer (the competition allows up to 3:00).
- "Graphics and gameplay must be representative of in-experience content."
- "You may start and end with your app logo, but don't use it throughout." "No logos from other platforms."
- "Consider using a mixture of traditional shots, mixed reality, and first person POV shots."
- 16:9, without letterboxing or pillarboxing.
- Recommended: Quest 3 footage "should include the text 'Captured on [device name].'"

**Meta's capture guide** (https://developers.meta.com/horizon/resources/video-capture-mr-vr/):
- Use a Quest 3 for mixed reality.
- In-headset recording is 1080p at about 36 fps (variable), 20 Mbps; the Meta VR Developer Hub app can record 2160p at 60.
- Image stabilization set to Low; slow head moves. You're "acting as the camera operator".
- Clear the room, storyboard the key moments, colour-correct.
- A single-player app is "probably limited to first person footage".

**Scout House on mixed-reality trailers** (https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/why-mixed-reality-capture-is-effective-qa-with-scout-house/): "Start your video off with mixed reality footage… grab the viewer within the first few seconds." In Meta's A/B tests, trailers with the player composited in raised conversions over POV-only ones (+30% for OhShape, +25% for Holopoint). For us that's a reason to weigh a third-person shot (concept B), not a rule.

## What transfers to a cozy mixed-reality storybook

1. **Open on the room changing, not on a logo or a menu.** Bricktales has the world on the table at 2 s, Nanauts the call in the hallway at 1 s. First Hand, What If and Starship Home spend their openings on logos or UI. Our beam crossing a real wall is exactly this.
2. **The Reader as a presence, not a face.** Moss never shows the player, and the bond still lands. In POV our hands are the presence: hold on a hand beside Pip.
3. **Hold when it's emotional; never cut a hand moment before it completes.** What If holds 36 s; First Hand cuts every interaction short. The lantern handback is one unbroken take.
4. **Let the cast speak; no narrator of ours.** Nanauts used only the app's voices and subtitles and won. Our book already narrates itself.
5. **One ordinary, real detail.** Nanauts' dog proves the room is real better than any effect. For us: a real window the beam crosses, a chair standing in the painted sea.
6. **A quiet ending.** Cubism ends on four seconds of silence. Name, link, "Captured on Meta Quest 3", and stop.
7. **Label honestly.** "Captured on Meta Quest 3", the way Starship Home does, fits the judges' "honestly represents".
8. **16:9, 1080p at least, stabilization low, slow heads.** Meta's own capture guide; stage 2 tests whether the Developer Hub's 2160p/60 capture works for a WebXR page.

## Clichés to avoid

- Logo stings first (What If's three cards, Starship Home's nine seconds).
- The smiling face in a headset, a fake "CLICK", a staged gasp.
- Slogan cards ("START BUILDING", "Greatness Awaits"), "Introducing…", "Imagine…", pre-order or "exclusive" lines.
- Hero narration over the footage.
- A feature label on every section (DreamSpace), or on any section.
- A montage of one-second cuts to pretend there's more than there is.
- Anything over 3:00, or anything copyrighted on screen.
